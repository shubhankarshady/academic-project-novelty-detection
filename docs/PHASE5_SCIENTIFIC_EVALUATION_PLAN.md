## 1. Executive Summary & Purpose

Until Phase 5.1, all similarity evaluations and model training in this platform were conducted against **synthetic benchmark labels** generated by automated rule-based templates. As established in the Phase 5.1 reliability audit, high model performance on synthetic labels merely proves that a model learned the synthetic rule function; it does **not** prove that the system reliably measures real-world academic novelty or similarity as judged by human expert panels.

This document formalizes the **Phase 5.2 Scientific Evaluation Methodology** to empirically evaluate the scoring models against independent human faculty consensus ratings.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Zero-Annotation Policy Guardrail**:  
> No empirical validation metrics (MAE, RMSE, Cohen's Kappa, Pearson $r$, or ranking precision) will be calculated or claimed until **genuine reviewer annotations** have been completed by faculty evaluators and adjudicated. All rows in `data/annotation/faculty_evaluation_sample_120.xlsx` are currently unannotated and marked as `PENDING_HUMAN_REVIEW`.

## 2. Evaluation Sample Design & Stratification

### 2.1 Sample Size and Dual-Batch Structure
A curated sample of **120 unique project pairs** was constructed via `scripts/generate_faculty_sample.py` (with deterministic random seed 42) and organized into **two independent worksheets of 60 pairs each** (`Evaluation_Batch_1` and `Evaluation_Batch_2`). This structure ensures manageable review loads while doubling statistical power:

| Split | Batch 1 | Batch 2 | Total Pairs | Purpose | Description |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Development / Calibration Set** (`DEV_CALIBRATION`) | **15** | **15** | **30** | Reviewer alignment & pilot calibration | 6 pairs per ordinal level (3 per batch). Used to calibrate reviewer understanding and align interpretations of edge cases before held-out evaluation. |
| **Held-Out Evaluation Set** (`HELD_OUT_EVALUATION`) | **45** | **45** | **90** | Unseen statistical benchmark | 18 pairs per ordinal level (9 per batch). Kept strictly held-out for double-blind human evaluation and final metric reporting. |
| **Total Evaluation Dataset** | **60** | **60** | **120** | Complete Study Cohort | Perfectly balanced across all 5 ordinal similarity levels ($24 \times 5 = 120$; $12 \times 5 = 60$ per batch). |

### 2.2 Stratification Across Ordinal Levels

The 120-pair sample guarantees uniform coverage across the full ordinal scale:

1. **Level 0 (Not Similar)**: 24 pairs total (12 in Batch 1 [3 Dev, 9 Eval], 12 in Batch 2 [3 Dev, 9 Eval]) — Cross-domain and unrelated problem formulations.
2. **Level 1 (Slightly Similar)**: 24 pairs total (12 in Batch 1 [3 Dev, 9 Eval], 12 in Batch 2 [3 Dev, 9 Eval]) — Shared domain jargon or general tooling, completely different research goals.
3. **Level 2 (Moderately Similar)**: 24 pairs total (12 in Batch 1 [3 Dev, 9 Eval], 12 in Batch 2 [3 Dev, 9 Eval]) — Core component overlap (e.g., shared dataset or algorithmic architecture) with divergent problems.
4. **Level 3 (Highly Similar)**: 24 pairs total (12 in Batch 1 [3 Dev, 9 Eval], 12 in Batch 2 [3 Dev, 9 Eval]) — Substantial conceptual convergence, strong paraphrases, matching research contributions.
5. **Level 4 (Nearly Duplicate / Duplicate)**: 24 pairs total (12 in Batch 1 [3 Dev, 9 Eval], 12 in Batch 2 [3 Dev, 9 Eval]) — Verbatim copies, near-duplicates, and minor cosmetic wording changes.

### 2.3 Integration and Allocation of Controlled Test Cases
To ensure rigorous benchmark coverage, the 8 documented controlled cases from `scripts/evaluate_semantic_feasibility.py` are balanced across both evaluation batches:

- **Batch 1 Controlled Cases (4 pairs)**:
  - `CASE_A_EXACT_COPY` (Verbatim copy $\implies$ Level 4, Dev)
  - `CASE_D_SAME_DOMAIN_DIFF_PROBLEM` (Domain overlap, distinct problem $\implies$ Level 1, Dev)
  - `CASE_E_DIFF_DOMAIN_COMMON_TECH` (Cross-domain common tools $\implies$ Level 0, Dev)
  - `CASE_F_SAME_DATASET_DIFF_OBJECTIVES` (Shared dataset, divergent goals $\implies$ Level 2, Eval)
- **Batch 2 Controlled Cases (4 pairs)**:
  - `CASE_B_LIGHT_PARAPHRASE` (Light wording edits $\implies$ Level 4, Eval)
  - `CASE_C_STRONG_PARAPHRASE` (Deep conceptual paraphrase $\implies$ Level 3, Eval)
  - `CASE_G_SAME_ALGO_DIFF_APPLICATION` (Shared algorithm architecture, different domain $\implies$ Level 1, Dev)
  - `CASE_H_SUPERFICIAL_WORDING_DIFF_OBJECTIVES` (Superficial keyword overlap, divergent goals $\implies$ Level 0, Dev)

### 2.4 Double-Blind Guardrail and Zero Data Leakage
- **Double-Blind Faculty Workbook**: Neither `Evaluation_Batch_1` nor `Evaluation_Batch_2` contains synthetic labels, category names, selection notes, or model scores. Reviewers evaluate pairs solely based on project text.
- **Zero Inter-Batch Overlap**: Every pair in Batch 1 and Batch 2 is distinct. No project pair (directed or undirected) appears in both batches.
- **Separation of Calibration and Evaluation**: Calibration and evaluation subsets remain disjoint, preventing information leakage.

## 3. Evaluation Methodology

Once faculty annotations are collected and adjudicated, the following statistical evaluation pipeline will be executed:

```
[Reviewer 1 Ratings]          [Reviewer 2 Ratings]
         │                             │
         └──────────────┬──────────────┘
                        │
                        ▼
           [Inter-Rater Reliability]
       (Quadratic Weighted Cohen's Kappa)
                        │
                        ▼
         [Adjudicated Ground Truth (y)]
                        │
         ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
         ▼              ▼              ▼
     [TF-IDF]       [Semantic]     [Hybrid]
     Model y_1      Model y_2      Model y_3
         │              │              │
         └──────────────┼──────────────┘
                        ▼
     [Comparative Statistical Evaluation]
    (MAE, RMSE, Pearson r, Spearman rho,
       Pairwise Concordance, Macro F1)
```

### 3.1 Inter-Rater Reliability (IRR) Analysis

To verify that human reviewers can distinguish similarity levels reliably, the following agreement metrics will be calculated between Reviewer 1 ($R_1$) and Reviewer 2 ($R_2$) across the 45 held-out evaluation pairs:

1. **Quadratic Weighted Cohen's Kappa ($\kappa_w$)**:  
   Standard Cohen's kappa penalizes all disagreements equally. For ordinal similarity ratings ($0 \le y \le 4$), a disagreement between 0 and 1 is far less severe than a disagreement between 0 and 4. Quadratic weighted kappa applies a quadratic penalty weight matrix:
   $$w_{ij} = 1 - \frac{(i - j)^2}{(k - 1)^2}$$
   $$\kappa_w = 1 - \frac{\sum_{i,j} w_{ij} O_{ij}}{\sum_{i,j} w_{ij} E_{ij}}$$
   *Acceptance Criteria*: $\kappa_w \ge 0.70$ (Substantial Agreement); $\kappa_w \ge 0.80$ (Near-Perfect Agreement).
2. **Linear Weighted Cohen's Kappa ($\kappa_{lin}$)**:  
   Applies absolute difference weighting $w_{ij} = 1 - \frac{|i - j|}{k - 1}$ for robust sensitivity analysis.
3. **Exact Percentage Agreement ($P_o$)**:  
   Percentage of pairs where $R_1 == R_2$.
4. **Adjacent Percentage Agreement ($P_{adj}$)**:  
   Percentage of pairs where $|R_1 - R_2| \le 1$.
5. **Krippendorff's Alpha ($\alpha$)**:  
   Calculated using ordinal distance metric to support potential multi-reviewer extensions.

### 3.2 Model Performance Evaluation Against Human Ground Truth

Each scoring model will be evaluated against the final **Adjudicated Human Ratings** ($y \in [0.0, 4.0]$).

#### Models Under Comparison:
1. **Production TF-IDF Model**: Fitted Ridge model over 8 lexical TF-IDF cosine features (`models/ridge_fusion_model.joblib`).
2. **Dense Semantic Embedding Model**: Mean dense cosine similarity across 8 sections using SentenceTransformer (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`) scaled to $[0.0, 4.0]$.
3. **Candidate Constrained Hybrid Fusion Model**: Convex combination ($\alpha = 0.50$) with SLSQP non-negative weights (`models/candidate_constrained_fusion_model.joblib`).

#### Evaluation Metrics:

1. **Continuous Error Metrics**:
   - **Mean Absolute Error (MAE)**:
     $$\text{MAE} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N |\hat{y}_i - y_i|$$
   - **Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE)**:
     $$\text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (\hat{y}_i - y_i)^2}$$
   - **Mean Bias Error (MBE)**: Evaluates whether a model systematically over-predicts or under-predicts similarity.
2. **Correlation Metrics**:
   - **Pearson Correlation Coefficient ($r$)**: Measures linear association between continuous predictions and human ratings.
   - **Spearman Rank Correlation ($\rho$)**: Measures monotonic rank order preservation.
3. **Pairwise Ranking Concordance (Kendall's $\tau$)**:  
   Measures whether for any two pairs $(A, B)$, if human experts rate Pair $A$ more similar than Pair $B$ ($y_A > y_B$), the model also predicts higher similarity ($\hat{y}_A > \hat{y}_B$).
4. **Decision Tier Classification (Macro F1 & Accuracy)**:  
   Mapping continuous scores to novelty decision tiers:
   - $[0.0, 1.0) \implies$ High Novelty
   - $[1.0, 2.0) \implies$ Moderate Novelty
   - $[2.0, 3.0) \implies$ Low Novelty
   - $[3.0, 4.0] \implies$ Very Low Novelty / Potential Duplicate
   Evaluated via Confusion Matrix, Weighted Precision, Recall, and Macro $F_1$.
